CSI-funded programme implementation · SOP 1.0 · September 2026

Make every Reflective session land.

Enablers are at the front of CSI-funded programmes. This playbook is the end-to-end operating standard: how we recruit, train, kickstart a lab, keep learners on the right journey, and prove that funded minutes turned into learning.

Role: ICT & Digital Platform Enabler Platforms: Numerate · Lingo · Mission Control Method: On-Track Gates Aligned to Training & Development 26 Mar 2026

Why this playbook exists

Existing training makes Enablers digitally ready. It does not yet show them, screen by screen, how to open a first session so a class of mixed-ability learners all start in the same place.

01

The gap

Enablers know Google Workspace, lab rules, and escalation. Learners still wander onto the wrong journey, start Data Literacy instead of Numbers, or sit frozen because the first five minutes were a flood of instructions.

02

The fix

A paced kickstart: one instruction, show the screen, wait until most learners are on track, then the next instruction. Plus a short training video and the checklists in this document.

03

The outcome

More time on app, honest assessments, the right first journey, and a funded CSI programme that can show logins, minutes, and mastery — not just devices in a room.

Field rule Never give the next instruction until most learners are on the same screen. Pace the room, do not race the clock.

The Enabler mandate

Reflective’s tools close hidden learning gaps — the iceberg under the Grade 8 or 9 mark. Numerate (Maths) and Lingo (English) diagnose those gaps and build a personal catch-up plan. CSI funding puts devices, connectivity, and youth Enablers into partner schools so that work actually happens. The Enabler is the implementation layer between a funded kit and a learner who is genuinely on the platform.

You do

Arrive early. Make the kit live. Hand out tablets and login cards. Kickstart with the script. Roam for technical snags. Re-engage distracted learners. Keep every child on the instructed journey. Read Mission Control. Log attendance, uptime, and wins. Escalate ICT issues to the ICT Manager and school/timetable issues to the School Success Manager.

You do not

Lead the class or replace the educator. Teach the Maths or English content — the app does that. Help during an assessment or allow a calculator. Handle discipline (that stays with the educator). Invent a different first journey because a learner “likes data”. Ignore a frozen screen because “they will catch up later”.

End-to-end process

One Enabler lifecycle, four phases. Session kickstart sits inside Phase 3 — that is where funded programmes succeed or stall.

P1

Source & recruit

SA Youth job card or school/community referral. Digital task + role-play. Police clearance and NRSO. ID and matric verified. Proximity to school is a design choice, not a nice-to-have.

P2

Train & certify

Six modules before and during contract. Enabler becomes the platform expert and completes Numerate and Lingo as a master learner. Certificate only after all six modules.

P3

Implement sessions

Morning prep. On-Track Gate kickstart. Roam, enable, do not teach. Sync data. This phase is the product the funder is paying for.

P4

Review & offboard

Weekly KPIs with the Enablement / ICT and Facilitation Manager. Handover of kit, passwords, and outstanding logs when a contract or school cycle ends.

How the programme works

Say this simply to learners. Numerate is not the day’s curriculum lesson. It finds the hidden gaps under the iceberg and fills them, concept by concept, up to Grade 9.

1. AssessmentDoes not count for marks. Shows the gaps.
→
2. Catch-upOnly the concepts the learner missed.
→
3. ReassessmentProve the concept is now mastered.

Then the platform opens the next gap. 81 key concepts sit across 7 journeys. Primary covers 68 concepts; secondary covers all 81. Typical use is about 60 minutes a week on a tablet or laptop with internet — more time accelerates catch-up.

The seven journeys

Numerate shows seven journeys — the building blocks of Maths up to Grade 9. Point at the live home screen and name them with the class. The first instructed journey is always Numbers. Do not allow a class to begin on Data Literacy or any other bubble because it looks interesting.

Session operating rhythm

Minimum standard: one successful session per class per week, at least 45 minutes. Enabler arrives 30 minutes early.

Kickstart protocol: On-Track Gates

Give one instruction. Show the screen. Wait until most learners are there. Then speak again. “Most” means roughly four out of five devices on the same step — not perfection, not the fastest child.

G0
Before learners

Lab is already on the login screen

Every tablet or laptop shows the Reflective login page. Login cards are stacked in seating order. Headphones checked. One device mirrored to the board or held up as the “show” device.

G1
SayShowWait

Tablet and login card

Hand out devices. Point at the login card. Once they are in, they will land on the home screen — then they wait. Do not continue while half the class is still typing a password.

G2
SayShowWait

Maths bubble

Click the Maths bubble. They will arrive on the journeys screen. Show your device. Wait until most screens match yours.

G3
SayWait

Explain the programme

Seven journeys. Maths up to Grade 9. Each journey starts with an assessment that does not count for marks. Truthful answers mean the app knows what to teach. Catch-up follows. Then a reassessment to prove mastery.

G4
SayWait

Assessment rules

No help from a friend, the Enabler, or the teacher. No calculator. No guessing to “get it over with”. Quiet room. Eyes on your own screen.

G5
SayShowWait

Open Numbers — only Numbers

The first assessment is the Numbers journey. Click Numbers and follow the prompts. If anyone is already in Data Literacy or another bubble, walk them back before the assessment starts.

Word-for-word learner script

Deliver in English, then repeat key lines in the room’s language (Afrikaans, isiXhosa, or isiZulu). Do not paraphrase the assessment rules.

Gate 1 — after cards are in hands
You will receive a tablet and a login card. Use only your card. Type the details carefully. Once you are logged in you will arrive at this screen. Then wait. Do not tap anything else yet.

Gate 2 — most learners on the home screen
Click the Maths bubble. You will arrive at this screen. Then wait again so we move together.

Gate 3 — journeys screen visible
You will see seven journeys. These are the sections that Maths up to Grade 9 is made of. Each section first asks you to do an assessment. This does not count towards your school marks. The assessment finds your gaps — the work from earlier grades that is still missing. Answer truthfully so we know what to teach you. After the assessment the app will guide you through catch-up. Then you will do another assessment to check that you have mastered those concepts.

Gate 4
Remember the rules of the assessment. No help from me, your teacher, or a friend. No calculator. Do your own thinking. If you guess, the app will teach you the wrong things and you will waste your time.

Gate 5
The first journey you will assess is Numbers. Click the Numbers section only. Follow the prompts on the screen. If you are on any other journey, come back to Numbers now. I will walk the room if a screen looks different.

After Gate 5 Stop talking. Roam. Kneel to the screen height. Fix logins and freezes. Point to the in-app Help. If a learner asks “is this right?”, answer: “During assessment I cannot help. Do what you honestly think.”

Training video: how Enablers kickstart a session

A short film plus this checklist closes the training gap. Shoot once in a real lab (or a staged lab that looks like one). Voice-over can be the ICT and Facilitation Manager; on-screen talent is an Enabler who already runs clean sessions.

Purpose

Show, do not only tell, the On-Track Gate method and the Numbers-first start. New Enablers watch before Module 3 classroom practice and again the morning of their first school day.

Length

9 to 11 minutes. Anything longer loses the field audience. Keep title cards short. Put the full script in this playbook, not on screen.

Lab management

Lab rules

No food or drink at devices. Assigned seats. Two hands on the tablet, not a neighbour’s screen. Report damage immediately. Login cards never photographed or shared on WhatsApp.

Enable, do not teach

If the question is “how do I divide fractions?”, the app teaches it. If the question is “the page is white” or “which bubble?”, that is you. Content questions during catch-up: “Read the explanation again, then try the next question.”

Re-engage

Stand near the off-task learner. Name the next action (“you should be on the Numbers assessment”). If they persist, the educator handles behaviour. Your KPI is minutes on the correct task, not winning an argument.

Troubleshooting tree

Fix what you can in the room. Do not wait for a technician while 30 learners lose a funded period.

Device will not show the login page

Power cable → battery → correct browser → refresh. If still dead, swap device from the spare pool and tag the failed unit in the issue log.

Learner cannot get onto the internet

Check whether other devices are online. If yes: that device’s Wi-Fi toggle, forgotten school network, or a blocked browser. If nobody is online: access point, ethernet to the router, then escalate as a site-wide outage to the ICT Manager.

Internet works but the platform errors

That is a server or account issue, not “no Wi-Fi”. Clear cache only if you were trained to, retry login, then escalate with the on-screen error text and time.

Login card fails

Caps lock, extra space, wrong card. Try once more with the learner watching. Then issue a replacement credential via the approved process — do not invent a shared class login.

Screen freeze mid-assessment

Wait 10 seconds, refresh once, log in again. Note the learner so Mission Control / support can check whether the attempt saved. Do not restart the whole class.

Reporting and weekly quality bar

Daily

Attendance headcount. Devices working vs booked. Connectivity note. Names of learners who never logged in. One success story when it is real — not invented.

Weekly huddle

With the Enablement or ICT and Facilitation Manager. Which gate leaked? Who is stuck on Mission Control? Which teacher still helps on assessments? What technique re-engaged the class?

KPI floor

80% of session time devices working. 80% of booked learners logged in and active. 90% of planned sessions delivered (≥45 minutes, one per class per week).

Training pathway (certificate)

Complete all six modules for the Reflective certificate. The kickstart video is a required artefact inside Modules 2 and 3 — it is the missing practical.

1. Getting Reflective ready

Google Workspace, browser management, cyber security, first Numerate and Lingo assessment plus catch-up before day one.

2. ICT tech support

Critical thinking, platform onboarding, Mission Control, connectivity, hardware, issue reporting. Watch the kickstart video here.

3. Classroom & learner management

Lab rules, Enabler vs teacher, engagement, rotations. Dry-run the On-Track Gates with peers before a live class.

4. Reporting & evaluation

Daily logs, progress flags, qualitative wins for CSI impact reports.

5. Workplace ethics

Professionalism, time, ethical decisions — including never sharing learner credentials.

6. Numeracy & finance

Finish Numerate as a master learner. Personal finance courses for life after the contract.

Common mistakes

Instruction flood

Explaining journeys, rules, and Numbers in one speech while half the class is still on the login box. Use the gates.

Wrong first journey

Learners self-select Data Literacy. Your job is to put the whole class on Numbers until that assessment-and-catch-up cycle is underway as instructed.

Helping on the test

A whispered hint corrupts the diagnostic. The app then builds the wrong catch-up. Kindness here is a programme failure.

Teaching the content

You become a second maths teacher and the software is unused. Enable access; the educator owns pedagogy; the app remediates gaps.

Late kit

Arriving with cold devices burns the kickstart window. The −30 habit is a KPI, not a courtesy.

Silent reporting

A rough session that is not logged cannot be fixed by HQ or shown to a CSI funder. Write the issue the same day.

Cut or screenshot · keep in the kit lid
